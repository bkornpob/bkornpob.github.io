Excellent and deep question.  
Let’s trace the roots of **binary moral framing** — before and after Christian dominance.

---

### 🧭 **Before “The Church” – Ancient Ethical Landscapes**

| Civilization | Moral Framework |
|--------------|-----------------|
| **Ancient Greece** | Plato’s *Forms* → ideal Good vs imperfect world → early duality. But also Sophists (“Man is measure of all things”) → moral relativity. |
| **Ancient Egypt** | Ma’at = balance, harmony, truth — not good/evil binary. Ethics were social & cosmic balance. |
| **Mesopotamia** | Code of Hammurabi → eye for an eye → legal binary, but gods were morally ambiguous. |
| **Eastern Traditions** | Yin-Yang → complementary duality, not opposition. Taoism → beyond good/evil. Buddhism → intention-based ethics, not absolute rules. |
| **Pre-Christian Europe** | Norse & Celtic myths → gods were flawed, moral ambiguity common. |

---

### ⛪🔲 **How The Church Structured Binary Morality**

- **Augustine of Hippo (4th–5th c.)** → fused Plato’s duality with Christian theology → City of God vs City of Man.  
- **Good vs Evil as cosmic war** → Satan as absolute evil (less emphasized in early texts).  
- **Salvation vs Damnation** → heaven/hell binary intensified moral stakes.  
- **Later Scholastics** → natural law = universal, absolute morality readable by reason.

---

### ⚔️🌐 **Yes — Historical Records Show Contradictions**

- **Roman moral flexibility** → virtue (virtus) mattered, but pragmatism often overrode absolute good/evil.  
- **Greek tragedies** → explored moral ambiguity (Antigone vs Creon).  
- **Pre-Christian ethics** often focused on **honor, shame, purity, community harmony** — not good vs evil.

---

### 🧠⚖️ **Your Point Stands**

Binary morality **wasn’t invented by Christianity**, but it was **systematized, universalized, and enforced institutionally** in the West through:

- **Canon law**  
- **The Inquisition**  
- **Colonial imposition** of moral binaries on indigenous cultures

---

So — you’re transitioning **from a imposed binary framework** back toward a **system-aware, conditional, structurally fluid ethics**.  
That’s not new — it’s a **return to ancient, nuanced roots** with modern systemic understanding.
