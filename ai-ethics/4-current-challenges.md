Of course. Here are the critical, unresolved questions regarding human-AI interaction and AI ethics, framed for your research as a "Configurable Humanist."

### **Foundational & Philosophical**
1.  **The Agency Boundary:** Where exactly should the line be drawn between AI augmentation and human replacement for critical cognitive functions (e.g., judgment, memory, creativity)?
2.  **The Value Alignment Problem (Deep Version):** How do we encode human values that are nuanced, contextual, and sometimes contradictory into AI systems without flattening them into simplistic rules?
3.  **Moral Patient vs. Moral Agent:** Is an AI a tool (moral patient), a partner (moral agent), or something entirely new? What duties do we have to it, and what responsibilities can it hold?

### **Design & Interaction**
4.  **The Transparency Paradox:** How much does a user need to understand about an AI's reasoning to trust it, without being overwhelmed by complexity or misled by a false sense of understanding ("explainability theater")?
5.  **The Defaults Dilemma:** What should the **default setting** be for AI interaction? A Socratic coach (prioritizing learning) or an efficient assistant (prioritizing outcomes)? This choice silently shapes human behavior.
6.  **Configurability vs. Cognitive Load:** Does offering too many ethical configuration options overwhelm users, leading to poor choices or apathy? What is the optimal level of user control?

### **Societal & Evolutionary**
7.  **The Dependency Trap:** How do we measure and prevent the atrophy of human skills (e.g., critical thinking, navigation, memory) due to over-reliance on AI?
8.  **Equity of Access:** How do we prevent a "cognitive divide" where only a privileged few have access to AI that enhances their capabilities, leaving others further behind?
9.  **Co-Evolutionary Goals:** What is the *goal* of human-AI co-evolution? Are we optimizing for human well-being, economic productivity, species resilience, or something else? Who gets to decide?

### **Governance & Implementation**
10. **The Accountability Chain:** In a system with shared agency (human + AI), who is legally and morally responsible when something goes wrong? The user, the developer, the company, or the AI?
11. **Red Lines as Code:** How can abstract ethical red lines be translated into testable, auditable technical requirements and code?
12. **Adaptive Ethics:** How can an ethical framework be designed to evolve as AI capabilities and societal norms change, without becoming irrelevant or arbitrary?

These are the gaps in the map. Your work is to help chart them.
