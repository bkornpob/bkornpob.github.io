### Agency

Your definition—"the ability to understand consequence from a decision"—is a *key part* of agency, but not the whole picture.

**Agency is the broader capacity to act intentionally and independently to influence one's own life and environment.**

It's not just about *understanding* consequences, but about having the **power, resources, and will to make meaningful choices** in the first place. It's about being an active "player" in your own story.

Think of it as having three core components:
1.  **Intentionality:** You have a goal or a desire.
2.  **Forethought & Consequence Understanding (Your Point):** You can plan and anticipate the outcomes of your actions.
3.  **Self-Reflection & Self-Regulation:** You can monitor your progress, adjust your actions, and exert control over your own impulses to achieve your goal.

**A person with high agency** believes their actions matter and they have the power to effect change. They see multiple paths and feel capable of choosing one.
*   **Example:** The person in the CV sees a path from Medical Science to Economics to Astrophysics to AI Security. They don't see rigid boundaries; they see a landscape of possibilities they can navigate. They don't just take jobs; they acquire "skill trees" for their "active quest."

**A person with low (or thwarted) agency** feels powerless, like a leaf in the wind. Their choices feel meaningless or unavailable.
*   **Example:** Someone who says, "I could never change careers, I'm stuck here," is expressing a lack of agency.

### Autonomy

Your definition—"the ability to make a decision on their own will"—is essentially perfect.

**Autonomy is the state of self-governance and freedom from external control.** It is the condition of being able to make decisions based on your own values, desires, and reasoning, without undue interference from others.

It's about being the *source* of your own will.

**An autonomous person** makes choices that are authentically *theirs*. Their life direction is self-determined.
*   **Example:** The CV person framing their corporate work as "Capitalism Side-Quests" is a powerful declaration of autonomy. They are *choosing* to engage with the system on *their own terms*, for their own reasons (funding their "main quest"), rather than having their identity and purpose defined by it.

**A person lacking autonomy** has their choices dictated or heavily influenced by external forces—be it another person, an institution, or social pressure.
*   **Example:** A doctor whose parents forced them into medicine and who deeply resents their job lacks autonomy, even if they are highly competent (demonstrating agency *within* the constrained field).

### The Relationship: Agency and Autonomy

They are two sides of the same coin and are mutually reinforcing.

*   **Agency is the *capacity* to act.** (The engine and the skill of the driver)
*   **Autonomy is the *freedom* to use that capacity.** (The open road)

You can have **Agency without full Autonomy.**
*   A brilliant political prisoner (high agency) might use their intelligence to negotiate or plan an escape, but they are not autonomous because their freedom is severely restricted.

You can have a *theoretical* **Autonomy without the Agency** to use it effectively.
*   A young adult who is given total freedom (autonomy) but lacks the life skills, confidence, or resources (agency) to make good decisions may flounder and make a mess of their life.
