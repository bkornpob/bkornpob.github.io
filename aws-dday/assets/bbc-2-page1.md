# Amazon web services return to 'normal operations' after mass outage, tech giant says

20 October 2025

![Flickr page saying site is down. A young girl has a replica panda on a lead](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1024/cpsprodpb/cc10/live/d326b9b0-ade2-11f0-aa13-0b0479f6f42a.jpg)

## Summary

- Amazon Web Services says it has fixed the underlying problem that has disrupted many of the world's biggest websites and apps, and that [all services have "returned to normal operations"](https://www.bbc.com/news/live/c5y8k7k6v1rt?post=asset%3Ad65be309-954b-4d25-96a5-841fa162bf44#post)
    
- Snapchat, Duolingo and Roblox were among those affected earlier, with platform outage monitor Downdetector saying [users are now reporting issues with Grok, Lyft and Hulu](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/live/c5y8k7k6v1rt?post=asset%3Ae5c8ce52-34c7-4847-8a50-0de37476ceec#post)
    
- Some applications came back online after more than six hours of disruption, while more than 1,000 companies were affected
    
- _The blame game for what happened to Amazon Web Services starts now,_ [our North America technology correspondent writes](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/live/c5y8k7k6v1rt?post=asset%3Ada98b850-c4f9-4fc4-8cdd-0e76d1fc6bb9#post)

    08:24 21 October
    
    We're wrapping up our live coverage of a mass outage that caused swathes of the internet to fall apart today.
    
    Amazon Web Services says it has fixed the underlying cause - but many of the world's biggest websites will be reeling from the outage which put them out of action for much of the day.
    
    The outage hit early today, and affected companies as varied as Snapchat, Roblox, Zoom, Coinbase, HMRC and [even banking services like Lloyds](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/live/c5y8k7k6v1rt?post=asset%3A4ef4cc37-f3f0-4252-adb6-11b43620739b#post), [Bank of Scotland and Halifax](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/live/c5y8k7k6v1rt?post=asset%3A23a91fb1-590b-4f94-a607-cd0d773ba3ca#post).
    
    The root of the problem was [a malfunction at Amazon Web Services](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/live/c5y8k7k6v1rt?post=asset%3A928cb10b-9412-4a6a-80ca-6e96c77bac92#post), the tech giant's cloud computing division, whose infrastructure underpins millions of large companies' websites and platforms.
    
    While outages affecting web platforms and services are nothing new, [multiple sites going down in one single outage are somewhat less common](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/live/c5y8k7k6v1rt?post=asset%3A4ad59492-eeab-4984-8add-4a452fed1212#post) - but experts say [they are becoming more frequent](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/live/c5y8k7k6v1rt?post=asset%3A607f5fcf-0161-4872-995d-4c429a7cfe3f#post), according to our technology reporter Liv McMahon.
    
    With much of our lives being online these days, the pressure on cloud services is only increasing all the time, writes technology reporter Shiona McCallum - and [events like these highlight the fragility of these systems](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/live/c5y8k7k6v1rt?post=asset%3A0b47aa62-2993-4819-bb35-2e3fa5e77965#post).
    
    AWS now says its cloud division has "returned to normal operations" and Downdetector reports of AWS issues have trailed off. [Now the blame game starts as companies start to try and recoup losses from today](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/live/c5y8k7k6v1rt?post=asset%3Ada98b850-c4f9-4fc4-8cdd-0e76d1fc6bb9#post), says our North America technology correspondent Lily Jamali.

2. ### What caused the outage?published at 08:17 21 October
    
    08:17 21 October
    
    Wondering why your ride-share app froze, digital wallet was not working, or a game failed to load on your server?
    
    Here is what we know about the cause of an outage that ground more than 1,000 companies' digital services to a halt:
    
    **First**, the outage originated in AWS's northern Virginia cluster, known as US-EAST-1, which is the world's largest cloud provider.
    
    It's the third time in five years a major internet outage has stemmed from the northern Virginia data centre.
    
    **Second**, we know the Domain Name System (DNS) is also the likely cause of the fault. [Likened to the internet's "phone book"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/live/c5y8k7k6v1rt?post=asset%3Ad24ec010-c2ee-4f0e-8c47-51d248d8a47b#post), the DNS can "paralyse" entire applications and services if it domain name resolution stops working, according to experts.
    
    We have been promised a detailed rundown by AWS of what went wrong, known as a "post-event summary", although that could take weeks or months.

4. ### 'It's always DNS' - engineer on major outagespublished at 06:22 21 October
    
    06:22 21 October
    
    An IT security officer at Thomas Cook's parent company says issues with a server's Domain Name System (DNS) typically triggers major outages.
    
    Marek Szustak, who works at eSky Group, explains that AWS's outage is to do with DNS - explained as the internet's 'phone book' - which is the "foundation of network communication".
    
    He says that when domain name resolution stops working, entire applications and services can come to a halt, no matter how well they are designed.
    
    Though a "seemingly minor piece of infrastructure", Szustak points out that DNS can "paralyse" the largest cloud environments.
    
    "As engineers say, it's always DNS," he says.
